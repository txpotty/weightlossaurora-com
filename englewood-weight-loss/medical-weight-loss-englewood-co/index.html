    How Medical Weight Loss Works in Englewood

    Medical weight loss treats excess weight as a chronic condition. Your provider looks for causes like insulin resistance, untreated sleep apnea, thyroid problems or medicines known to add weight before building a plan. Medication may or may not be part of it. When medication fits, semaglutide and tirzepatide are frequent picks because they reduce appetite.

    Who Medical Weight Loss Is For

    Adults who have not reached a healthy weight with diet and exercise alone are the usual fit. Prescription medicines are generally considered for a BMI of 30 or more, or 27 or more with a weight-related condition like high blood pressure, type 2 diabetes, high cholesterol or sleep apnea. Coaching works below those cutoffs. Medication is not used during pregnancy.

    Treatment Schedule in Englewood

    Week one is the intake and review. A weekly injection plan steps up every four weeks, while a daily pill plan usually means one tablet each morning, sometimes building over a month. Early follow-ups are frequent. Stable patients often move to check-ins every month or two.

    What to Expect Week by Week

    The first two weeks focus on habits and, if prescribed, a starting dose. Many people on GLP-1 medicine notice less hunger within the first week or two, and mild stomach symptoms show up most after starting and after each dose increase. Expect slow, steady change. A loss of 1 to 2 pounds a week is a common target once a plan is underway, though medication plans vary.

    Side Effects in Englewood

    Side effects depend on the treatment. GLP-1 medicines commonly cause nausea, diarrhea, constipation and reduced appetite, while oral appetite medicines can cause dry mouth, trouble sleeping or a faster heart rate. Gallstones are a known risk. Your provider will push protein and strength training to limit muscle loss.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Screening covers your BMI, conditions, medications and weight history. Certain medicines are ruled out by a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2, uncontrolled high blood pressure, pregnancy or a history of eating disorders. Labs may be requested. Common asks are A1C, a lipid panel, thyroid tests and a metabolic panel from the past year.

    Medical Weight Loss Compared With Other Approaches in Englewood

    Commercial diet programs cannot prescribe. They focus on food and behavior, while medical weight loss adds a licensed provider who can treat appetite hormones, manage related conditions and adjust medications that may be adding weight. Treatment options include FDA-approved injections, oral medicines and compounded versions that lack FDA approval. Surgery remains an option for some.
